Analytics audit

Security · Privacy · Principal engineering · Trust

ugo — open-source URL shortener with first-party click analytics (hosted at ugo.cr or self-hosted via Once/Docker).

This deck audits how trustworthy analytics are for link owners (customers) and what risks exist for people who click shortened links, plus operator/hosted-service concerns. Based on README, docs/*, static marketing pages, and the visit pipeline in application code (May 2026).

Executive summary

Bottom line: ugo’s analytics are credible for aggregate campaign insight (volume, geo, device mix) with a privacy-conscious design on the click path (no third-party trackers, no cookies on redirects, daily-rotating pseudonymous hashes). They are not a forensic or identity-grade analytics product—and marketing that says “see who clicks” overpromises relative to what the product stores and displays.

AudienceTrust / risk posture
Link owners (paying customers) Moderate–good for trends; expect under/over-counting at edges (bots, limits, hash rotation).
Link clickers (public) Moderate exposure: IP, UA, referrer, coarse geo stored; no cookies, but data visible to link owner’s team and instance operator.
Hosted ugo.cr operator High responsibility: plaintext visit rows, SQLite backups, GDPR-style duties for EU clickers unless self-host shifts control.

Analytics architecture

Click GET /:slug → LinksController#link (minimal DB reads) → after_action track_visit → RecordVisitJob (Solid Queue, separate SQLite) → Visit row: ip_address, user_agent, referer, timestamp, visitor_hash → ProcessVisitsJob (every ~10s): GeoLite2, DeviceDetector, CrawlerDetect → enrich OR destroy (bot / plan limit / blocked workspace) → VisitsController: aggregates + 60m cache → ApexCharts / DataMaps UI
  • Hot path is intentionally thin: redirect does not synchronously write to the primary DB—good for reliability and latency.
  • Only processed visits (processed_at set) appear in dashboards—transient rows exist between click and job run.
  • Charts are computed in Ruby over filtered scopes (day/week/month), not a separate OLAP store—simple, inspectable, SQLite-bound at scale.

What is collected per click

Stored on every recorded visit

  • IP address (plaintext)
  • User-Agent string
  • HTTP Referer (nullable)
  • Timestamp
  • Link ID
  • visitor_hash (HMAC, daily salt)

Added after processing

  • Country, region, city (MaxMind GeoLite2)
  • Lat/long + accuracy radius
  • Device type, browser, OS

Not collected on redirect

  • No cookies on the public redirect page (matches privacy policy).
  • No third-party analytics scripts on links/link.html.erb—only meta tags + JS redirect to destination.

Collected but not shown in UI

  • Referrer is persisted; dashboards show visitors, geo, devices only—marketing mentions “referrers” but the product does not surface them yet.
  • Raw IP is not exposed in the analytics UI to workspace members (still in DB/backups/console).

Unique visitors & pseudonymity

VisitorHash follows the Plausible/Fathom pattern: HMAC-SHA256(IP + UA + link_id) keyed with secret_key_base and a UTC daily salt.

Privacy positives

  • No cross-site tracking cookie.
  • Hashes differ per link—no global visitor ID across a workspace.
  • Yesterday’s hashes cannot be recomputed after salt rotation.

Accuracy & trust caveats

  • “Unique visitors” for week/month counts distinct hashes in-range—same person across UTC days appears as multiple uniques.
  • Shared NAT / corporate networks collapse many users into one hash.
  • UA rotation (browser updates) splits one person into multiple hashes.
  • With IP blank, hash is nil—excluded from unique count.

Principal engineer note: rename or footnote “unique visitors” in UI for ranges > 1 day, e.g. “estimated uniques (daily buckets)”.

Trustworthiness for link owners

Will the numbers support business decisions (campaign ROI, geo targeting, device share)?

  • Strengths: First-party data; bot visits removed via CrawlerDetect before dashboards; workspace RBAC (WorkspaceAuthorization) gates analytics; chart cache invalidated on new processed visits.
  • Under-counting: When monthly event limits are hit, new visits are deleted during processing—not queued—silently reducing totals. Blocked post-trial workspaces discard visits similarly.
  • Over-counting: Interstitial page fires tracking on HTML render; prefetchers, link expanders, email scanners, and health checks may register clicks before bot filter runs. No redirect rate limiting.
  • Stale cache: Chart payloads cached 60 minutes—near–real-time campaigns can look laggy.
  • Missing product surface: Referrer breakdown promised in copy but not shipped—owners may duplicate tagging with UTM + external analytics anyway (by design in URL builder).
Good: trends & comparisons Caution: absolute counts Poor: per-person identity

Security & privacy risks for link clickers

People who click ugo.cr/xxxxx (or a self-hosted short domain) are not ugo account holders but are still data subjects.

  • Transparency: Privacy policy (Feb 2026) discloses IP, UA, referrer, coarse location—accurate. Clickers never see a just-in-time notice on the redirect interstitial.
  • Re-identification risk: IP + city + UA + referrer can identify individuals in small populations or leak sensitive context (e.g. referrer URL with tokens—stored if browser sends it).
  • Data controller split: On ugo.cr, FDO CR / operator is controller; link owner is processor-like in practice but policy frames analytics for “link owners.” Self-hosted instances: deployer is controller—policy correctly scoped.
  • Destination still learns: Final URL receives the user directly; ugo analytics are additive to whatever the destination site tracks.
  • Open redirect abuse: Short links can mask phishing targets; ugo is not a safe-browsing filter—reputational risk to clickers who trust the short domain.
  • Slug entropy: ~365 slugs with random generation—enumeration/bruteforce of active links is plausible at scale without rate limits on redirects.

Privacy policy vs implementation

Aligned

  • No cookies on clickers; session cookie for accounts only.
  • No third-party ad trackers on redirect.
  • Plan-based retention (cleanup job: 1 mo free, 3 mo paid).
  • TLS forced in production; bcrypt passwords.

Gaps to close

  • Policy: data “tied to the link, not to an individual”—technically true in UI, but IP is PII stored per row.
  • Policy: “sensitive fields encrypted at rest”—visit IPs/geo are not encrypted (encryption used for SMTP, API keys, etc.).
  • Marketing: “see who clicks” vs aggregate analytics—misleading for GDPR-conscious buyers.
  • Hosted app layout allows admin_scripts (raw HTML)—site admin can inject third-party scripts on authenticated pages, contradicting “no third-party trackers” for app users if misused.

Subprocessors: MaxMind GeoLite2 (offline DB), Polar (billing), email SMTP, Honeybadger (optional), hosting/Once backups—document in a DPA/subprocessor list for enterprise sales.

Platform security (analytics-relevant)

  • Authorization: Analytics routes require workspace membership; admin panel is site-admin only—appropriate separation.
  • API: GET /api/links/:id returns slug/url only—no visit export via API (reduces exfiltration channel, limits integrations).
  • Rate limiting: Only login is throttled—redirect and visit ingestion are unbounded (DoS / cost / analytics poisoning).
  • Logs: Production log level defaults to info; comment warns PII at debug. IP not in parameter filter list—ensure jobs/controllers never log raw visit params.
  • Backups: Once/SQLite snapshots contain full visit table—protect backup storage as strongly as production.
  • Console access: docker exec … rails console on self-host = full IP visibility—document for operators.
  • Host authorization: Commented out in production—rely on reverse proxy; enable per-tenant domains when custom domains ship.

Self-hosted & dedicated deployments

Self-hosting shifts trust from ugo.cr to the operator—often a feature, not a bug.

  • Data sovereignty: All visit rows stay on operator disk; no mandatory third-party analytics SDK.
  • Threat model change: Insider risk moves to customer IT; they must patch, backup keys (ACTIVE_RECORD_ENCRYPTION_*), and harden Docker/SSH.
  • License (O'Saasy): Cannot offer competing hosted SaaS on the same codebase—reduces forked “privacy ugo” hosts but strengthens ugo.cr moat.
  • Accuracy: Operators who skip GeoLite2 download get visits stuck unprocessed—dashboards stay empty (documented in quickstart).

Recommendations (priority)

Trust & accuracy

  • Ship referrer breakdown or remove from marketing.
  • Clarify “unique visitors” for multi-day ranges.
  • Surface “events dropped (limit)” to admins.
  • Consider Referrer-Policy / strip sensitive referrers at ingestion.

Privacy & compliance

  • Encrypt or hash IP at rest; shorten retention for IP after geo enrichment.
  • Align privacy copy with actual encryption scope.
  • Optional redirect notice: “Anonymous stats collected.”
  • Publish subprocessor list + DPA template.

Security

  • Rate-limit GET /:slug per IP/slug.
  • Audit logging for admin access to visit data.
  • Restrict admin_scripts or CSP-harden.

Scale path

  • SQL aggregations vs loading rows in Ruby for charts.
  • Separate visit store if multi-tenant traffic grows.

SWOT — project & analytics positioning

Strengths

  • First-party, auditable analytics pipeline—no GA dependency.
  • Privacy-forward click path (no cookies, daily hashes, bot filtering).
  • Open source + self-host story for regulated / EU teams.
  • Integrated UTM builder + short links + team workspaces.
  • Modern Rails 8 ops (Solid Queue/Cache, SQLite simplicity).

Weaknesses

  • Measurement gaps: referrers missing, unique visitor semantics, silent cap drops.
  • Plaintext IP storage vs privacy marketing.
  • SQLite + Ruby aggregation ceiling for hosted multi-tenant scale.
  • Thin API—hard to pair with data warehouse / BI.
  • O'Saasy license limits competing hosted forks.

Opportunities

  • “Ethical shortener” niche vs Bitly + Google Analytics.
  • Enterprise: IP redaction, SSO, SLA, EU hosting, export API.
  • Referrer + funnel charts without third-party scripts.
  • Certifications / privacy seals if IP retention tightened.
  • Agency white-label via AppConfig branding cleanup.

Threats

  • Regulatory scrutiny of IP-as-personal-data (GDPR, ePrivacy).
  • Phishing/malware via short links damaging brand trust.
  • Commodity free tiers (Bitly, Rebrandly) with mature analytics.
  • Customers expecting GA-level fidelity and real-time accuracy.
  • Operator breach of SQLite backups = full click history leak.

Decision guide for leadership

  • Double down on privacy-first analytics if you can close the IP/referrer/unique-visitor gaps—this is the clearest differentiator vs incumbents.
  • Position hosted ugo.cr for teams who want convenience; position self-host for teams who must own the controller role—same code, different trust contract.
  • Do not compete on hyper-scale or identity resolution without architectural investment; win on transparency, data minimization, and integrated link workflow.
  • Before enterprise sales: subprocessor list, retention matrix, optional IP hashing, and honest marketing copy are table stakes.

Overall grade (analytics trust): B — thoughtful foundation with known accuracy and compliance sharp edges worth fixing before high-stakes customers.
